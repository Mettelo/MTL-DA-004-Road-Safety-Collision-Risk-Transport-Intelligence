## 1. Client Situation

The client is responsible for supporting road-safety strategy, transport planning and targeted intervention across a large regional area.

Senior stakeholders receive periodic reports on collisions and casualties, but existing reporting is largely descriptive and fragmented. Teams often look at collisions, vehicles and casualties separately, making it difficult to understand the full circumstances of severe incidents.

Leadership wants a more integrated analytical view to identify where serious harm is concentrated, which road users are most exposed, what environmental and vehicle characteristics are associated with severity, and where further investigation or intervention should be prioritised.

## 2. Business Problem

The organisation does not currently have one reproducible analytical layer connecting:

- collision circumstances;
- vehicles involved;
- casualties;
- road and junction characteristics;
- lighting and weather conditions;
- geographic location;
- road-user type;
- casualty severity;
- driver and casualty characteristics.

The project team has been commissioned to develop a governed analytical solution that supports evidence-led road-safety planning.

## 3. Project Objectives

### A. Establish a trusted analytical foundation
Profile and validate the collision, vehicle and casualty datasets and document important limitations.

### B. Build an integrated analytical model
Create reproducible relationships between collisions, vehicles and casualties without double counting.

### C. Define road-safety KPIs
Develop clear measures for collision frequency, severity, KSI outcomes, casualty profiles and high-risk conditions.

### D. Identify risk concentrations
Investigate where and when severe outcomes are concentrated across geography, road environment and road-user groups.

### E. Support vulnerable-road-user analysis
Assess patterns affecting pedestrians, pedal cyclists, motorcyclists and other vulnerable groups.

### F. Produce decision-support outputs
Provide management with a clear analytical product, evidence summary and prioritised areas for further investigation.

## 4. Key Business Questions

1. Where are killed-or-seriously-injured outcomes concentrated geographically?
2. Which road-user groups experience the most severe outcomes?
3. How do collision patterns vary by time of day, day of week and season?
4. What road, junction, speed-limit, lighting or weather characteristics are common in severe collisions?
5. Which vehicle types are disproportionately represented in serious incidents?
6. How do age and sex patterns differ across casualty groups?
7. Which locations or circumstances show persistent risk over several years?
8. What metrics should management monitor routinely?
9. Which findings are robust enough to inform intervention prioritisation?
10. What important limitations in STATS19 should constrain interpretation?

## 5. Expected Analytical Work

Teams are expected to:

- profile all source files;
- validate keys and relationships;
- distinguish collision-, vehicle- and casualty-level grain;
- map coded fields using official documentation;
- build reusable transformation logic;
- create severity and KSI measures;
- conduct temporal and geographic analysis;
- assess vulnerable road users;
- investigate contextual risk patterns;
- avoid misleading denominators or unsupported causal claims;
- build a management-facing dashboard/product;
- provide technical handover documentation.

## 6. Constraints

- STATS19 covers personal-injury collisions reported to police on public roads;
- not all collisions are represented;
- reporting practices and variable definitions can change over time;
- raw source data must remain unchanged;
- location data must be handled responsibly;
- observational associations must not be presented as proof of causation;
- teams must use official code/value guidance where variables are encoded;
- recommendations must clearly distinguish evidence from hypothesis.

## 7. Success Criteria

The engagement will be successful where the team can demonstrate:

- correct understanding of data grain;
- reliable linkage across collisions, vehicles and casualties;
- reproducible transformations;
- clear and defensible KPIs;
- meaningful geographic and temporal analysis;
- careful treatment of vulnerable-road-user findings;
- responsible interpretation;
- a useful stakeholder-facing product;
- sufficient technical documentation for handover;
- visible contribution evidence from team members.

## 8. Final Review

The team will present to a Mettelo review panel acting as the client's senior stakeholder group.

The panel may challenge:

- join logic;
- denominator choice;
- KSI definition;
- hotspot methodology;
- geographic interpretation;
- trend comparison;
- handling of coded values;
- recommendations;
- limitations.
